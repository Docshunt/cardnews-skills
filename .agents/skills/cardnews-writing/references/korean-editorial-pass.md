# Korean editorial pass for card news

Do this after the script and facts are locked, before copy enters the layout. It improves reading rhythm without changing evidence or trying to disguise authorship.

## Protect before editing

Never change:

- names, dates, numbers, units, programme names, sources, and URLs;
- direct quotations or legally meaningful wording;
- the distinction between observed fact and editorial interpretation;
- the slide's claim, narrative role, or visual instruction.

## Detect only local style problems

Mark the smallest affected phrase or sentence.

- translation-like phrasing such as `~를 통해`, `~에 있어서`, double passives, or ornamental English;
- automatic fillers such as `결론적으로`, `시사하는 바가 크다`, `주목할 만하다`, and `중요한 점은`;
- repeated sentence openings, three identical endings, or a uniformly clipped rhythm;
- needless connectors at the start of consecutive slides (`또한`, `따라서`, `한편`, `즉`);
- lists, questions, bolding, or quotation marks used as decoration rather than to advance the swipe.

## Rewrite for cards

- Use a direct subject and verb. Delete a transition if the page order already shows the relation.
- Keep the existing short-line rhythm. Vary the cadence across pages, not within one claim until it becomes prose.
- Keep a list only when it helps the reader compare criteria or perform a sequence. Otherwise turn it into one confident sentence.
- Preserve ambiguity when the source is uncertain. Do not add urgency or certainty just to make a line punchier.

## Standard and strict modes

Use the standard pass for a normal issue: detect, make minimal edits, then compare every changed line to the source script.

Use **strict** mode for long explanatory issues, support-programme or policy content, sensitive claims, or a user request. List protected facts first; make a finding list; revise only marked spans; then independently check source fidelity and page rhythm. If an optional `humanize-korean` skill is installed, it may perform this pass. Its absence must not block the card-news workflow.
